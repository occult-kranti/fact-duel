# Pocket Stadium

Replaces the Gully Club poster world on 29 October 2026. A mobile cricket clubhouse: a cinematic ground in daylight or under floodlights, decisive thumb-sized play, playful public-record drama, and a personally built miniature stadium.

The home is Experience with a single Operate action. The live match is Operate: stable answer positions, fixed question layout, high contrast, immediate lock, clock and receipt. Decoration and physics never alter quiz scores. The club is a readable collection/shop surface, with ownership, price and point source visible before spending.

## Visual language

Day: mist-white #eff3e8, turf ink #142e28, white surfaces, muted green borders. Night: midnight #071b22, dark teal #102b32, white-green text. Primary play is electric lime #d0fb70 on dark green. Spicy uses warm apricot and burnt clay. Bricolage Grotesque headlines, Instrument Sans controls, JetBrains Mono scoreboard annotations stay self-hosted.

Original generated miniature stadium art is embedded as compressed local WebP, backed by a plain-colour surface if absent. Day/night changes the sky, lights and UI together. CSS depth and an optional physical ball trajectory make Net practice tangible. “4D” is interpreted as depth plus interaction over time; there is no claim of literal four-dimensional rendering, sensor hardware or immersive haptics.

Home contains one cricket start, one Spicy start, human/friend entry, My club, quick single, Net practice and exploration. Mobile has a compact stadium above a 55px primary action. Desktop uses a wider ground beside the match desk. Online activity sits directly below human-entry buttons: actual aggregate human profiles active within two minutes, never invented audience counts or a promise of a free rival. Missing/stale service data explicitly becomes unavailable.

## Gameplay and identity

One click launches five zero-stake cricket questions at30seconds each, across substantive difficulties. Cricket101/law-recall questions are centrally excluded from local and server new deals. Spicy uses an exact subtopic pool of24 source-backed public-record cards; dates and appeal outcomes distinguish historical sanctions from current status. Answers always carry their source context.

Titles, card finishes and three stadium-building slots use a separate free Club-point balance earned from device-local completed matches and correct answers. Owned/equipped state is explicit and persisted transactionally. Time-based badges use eligible recent actual journal results, visible expiry and no server-ranking claim. Human coins, stakes and competitive server records remain separate. Copied status cards are user-controlled share text.

Preset cricket banter can be sent to another human through the authenticated server. Eight-second authority cooldown, phase gating, strict allowlist and mute support keep it bounded. Bots are labelled and use a local reply. No free text, identity slurs, or answer-covering effects. Physics celebrations happen only after settled results.
