THE PROBLEM — THE VACUUM

A format with hundreds of millions of lifetime installs, and essentially no maintained incumbent.

  • Trivia Crack: 267,939,837 lifetime Android installs per Google Play's internal store data, displayed publicly as 100M+ — installs, not users.
  • Its Play listing is now retitled around a FOX TV tie-in: the largest lifetime install base in the category has left the duel.
  • QuizDuel's owner MAG Interactive: nine-month average group DAU -14% and MAU -13% YoY (Sep 2025 - May 2026); for Q3 alone, -12% and -9%.
  • QuizDuel itself is live and maintained — iOS build updated 10 Sep 2026 — and tiny in the US: 3.51 stars from 78 US iOS ratings.
  • Everything else marketing itself as live 1v1: Quizion, 10+ downloads (updated 13 Aug 2026); TRIVIA GO!, 10K+, no update since 22 May 2025.
  • The format proved demand at scale and has essentially no maintained incumbent. That gap is the entire reason to keep reading.
Speaker notes
CRV asks for a traction teaser in the first two or three slides. We have no traction, so the credibility hook has to be evidence instead: this slide plus slide 3. Every line is store data or a filed interim report, verified 13 Sep 2026, not an analyst estimate. Caveats to volunteer rather than be caught on: 267,939,837 is Google Play's own internal lifetime install count and the store itself displays only '100M+'; it is installs, not users; and the widely-quoted '600 million downloads' for Trivia Crack is PR-originated and does not survive checking (numbers.md §5.4), so we deliberately use the smaller, verifiable number. 03

THE GRAVEYARD

It did not die of distribution. It died of monetisation and long-tail retention.

  • Trivia Royale: 2.5M downloads in about three weeks on a launch budget under $500,000 — a blended $0.20 or less per download, organic and viral included. Company-reported to TechCrunch, not audited.
  • 45% day-one iOS retention, around 30 minutes of use a day — both company-reported to TechCrunch, not audited. Closed eight months after launch.
  • HQ Trivia: 2.38M peak concurrent (28 Mar 2018), then roughly 67,000 monthly installs in Jan 2020, about 3% of the ~2M/month Feb 2018 rate (Sensor Tower estimates).
  • It did not end there: bought and relaunched 29 Mar 2020, last official game 17 Nov 2022, removed from both stores 5 Aug 2023.
  • QuizUp: about $32.4M of equity raised; sold to Glu in 2016 for $7.5M of cancelled notes, booked at $3.2M fair value.
  • Near-free acquisition and elite D1 were necessary and demonstrably not sufficient. Distribution was never the binding constraint here — monetisation and long-tail retention were.
Speaker notes
This is the slide that decides whether an investor trusts the rest of the deck. Trivia Royale is the cleanest natural experiment in the category: built by QuizUp's own founder, it took 2.5M downloads at a blended $0.20 or less each on a launch budget under $500,000 (about 40% of it to TikTok influencers), it reported 45% day-one iOS retention, and it was gone in eight months because revenue was insufficient and funding talks collapsed. Caveats that belong on the page and not only here: the download count, the budget and the 45% D1 are all Teatime Games statements to TechCrunch — press reports, not filings, not audited; the $0.20 is blended cost per download including organic and viral, not a paid CPI; and both HQ Trivia install figures are rounded Sensor Tower modelled estimates, which is why the slide says about 3% rather than a ratio to three significant figures. Do not present 45% D1 as a percentile placement against a benchmark: it is iOS-only and company-reported, while the GameAnalytics medians are measured on iOS and Android app-store installs, so the comparison is not like-for-like (numbers.md §5.14). HQ Trivia's story does not stop in February 2020 — the franchise was bought, relaunched on 29 Mar 2020, played its last official game on 17 Nov 2022 and was delisted from both stores on 5 Aug 2023 — say so before an investor who knows the history says it for you. Glu's $3.2M fair-value mark on QuizUp is the honest ceiling to attach to a large unmonetised trivia audience, and the ~$32.4M of Plain Vanilla equity behind it comes from the SEC filings. The takeaway to say out loud: any pitch in this category that leads with 'we will go viral' is arguing the case that was already settled and ignoring the case that killed everyone. If asked what we would do differently, do not answer with features — answer with slide 4.

THE SOLUTION

So the bet is sequencing: build the habit loop first, prove retention, then monetise.

  • GameAnalytics estimates the median mobile game session at 3.1-3.5 min and North America at 3.64 min (CY2025, iOS and Android installs — not web).
  • A duel is designed as a 3-4 minute unit. The app now records sessions and engaged time, but with no players we have measured no session length of our own (numbers.md §5.15, §7.1).
  • Around the duel, product description rather than audited fact: XP and levels, day streaks, daily quests, achievements, a device-local rank, gems, cosmetics.
  • Solo expeditions and a static event calendar are intended to cover the days when nobody wants a live opponent. No retention benefit has been measured.
  • Zero revenue today (numbers.md status paragraph, §5.15). Monetisation is sequenced last by choice, and that choice is unproven.
  • The comparables on slide 3 failed on monetisation and long-tail retention. Retention is the gate; monetisation is downstream of a number we do not have.
Speaker notes
The session-length line is a design argument, not a traction claim, and the slide now says so on its face: GameAnalytics' 2026 benchmark set (16,000+ games, nine regions, iOS and Android, calendar year 2025) estimates the median session at 3.1-3.5 minutes and North America at 3.64 minutes across 4.23 sessions a day — all of it measured on app-store installs rather than on the web, which numbers.md §5.14 calls the single largest methodological risk in the model. We have measured no session length of our own: since 13 September 2026 the app records session count and engaged time per device (lib/analytics.mjs, numbers.md §7.1), but there are no players, so there is nothing to report — the recorder exists, the sample does not. The loop around the duel is product description taken from our own repository, not an audited inventory and not a retention claim: do not attach a retention benefit to any of these mechanics, because we have not measured one: the instrument that would measure it shipped on 13 September 2026, and what is missing now is players rather than the ability to count them. Nothing in the evidence base shows that deferring monetisation avoids this category's failure mode — §2.3 says the comparables died of monetisation and long-tail retention, and sequencing is our response to that, untested. The honest claim is sequencing discipline, not novelty: we are copying a known-good loop rather than inventing one. Files an investor can open for the product description: lib/progression.mjs, lib/expeditions.mjs, lib/events-data.mjs, app/screens/play/opponent-picker.tsx.

TRACTION — STATED PLAINLY

Zero users. Zero revenue. No cohort. The instrument that measures retention is built, tested and shipped — the round buys the cohort to point it at.

  • No users outside a private playtest, no revenue, no cohort, 54 sample questions. Nothing on this page is a traction claim.
  • Shipped 13 Sep 2026: install day, active days, sessions, engaged time and per-device D1/D7/D30, with a player-facing /analytics screen, JSON and CSV export and erase (24 tests, numbers.md §7.1).
  • Also shipped: an opt-in anonymous daily ping whose report is aggregate-only, with buckets under five devices suppressed — a cohort rate becomes computable the day there are players (20 tests).
  • One device can only ever answer yes, no, or not-yet. A rate needs a cohort; that is what is missing, and that is what the round buys.
  • Milestone 1 existed because nothing was measurable without accounts. Measurement landed without them, so milestones 1 and 2 are de-risked from build-then-measure to simply run the cohort.
  • Milestone 2 — publish D1/D7/D30 against GameAnalytics estimates (16,000+ mobile games, iOS and Android, CY2025): NA medians 23.28% / 4.97% / 1.18%.
  • Target — beat that 4.97% NA median D7 on a cohort of at least 1,000 players. The benchmark is measured on app-store installs and we are a web app, so transfer is unproven.
  • Milestones 4-6 are untouched: grow the bank from 54 questions, ship an opponent-integrity answer before monetisation, validate exactly one monetisation route.
Speaker notes
DocSend's finding is that VCs spend 80 percent more time evaluating the traction of companies that didn't raise money successfully: a vague traction slide invites the inspection that kills the round, so the honest move is to state zero and move on. Say it in one sentence and do not soften it — no waitlist presented as users, no playtest presented as a cohort, no pilot presented as revenue, and the shipped measurement layer presented as a capability rather than a result. What changed on 13 September 2026 is documented in numbers.md §7.1 and it retires this deck's previous claim that retention was unmeasurable in principle: the app now records install day, active days, session count, engaged time and four in-app counters per device and computes that device's own D1/D7/D30, returning true, false, or null while a window is still open, so an unfinished window can never read as a miss; there is a player-facing /analytics screen with JSON and CSV export and erase; and an opt-in anonymous daily ping (cohort-ping / cohort-report) makes a cohort rate computable, with an aggregate-only report, no per-device row, and every bucket describing fewer than five devices suppressed. BUSINESS MODEL

We copy the habit subscription, not trivia IAP — and here is the cross-check that keeps us honest.

  • Trivia IAP is the dead end: Sensor Tower estimates the top three true trivia apps on US iOS at ~$102.5K in their peak weeks of Q2 2025, and ~$90K by quarter end.
  • Those are modelled estimates of gross consumer spend before Apple's 15-30% cut, not audited or company-reported revenue — and they are Q2 2025, not current.
  • Habit subscriptions are not the dead end: Duolingo took $1,037.6M of FY2025 revenue at a 9.17% FY2025 MAU-to-paid ratio (12.2M of 133.1M).
  • Kahoot collected $146.0M in FY2022 at a 95% gross margin — but from B2B/education subscriptions, and FY2022 is its last comparable year before going private.
  • Our cross-check at base-case Year-3 scale (P): 105,900 MAU x 9.03% x $43.99 = $420,666 (P). The install-economics route gives $1,630,517 (P) — 3.88x more.
  • So we present $0.4M - $1.6M (P). $43.99 is Sporcle's annual price; 9.03% is derived from Duolingo's filed subscriber and MAU counts at 30 Jun 2026. Neither is ours.
  • That base-case scale (P) rests on chosen inputs: Year-3 install volume and 20% monthly churn have no company data behind them.
Speaker notes
This is the longest-dwell slide in a seed deck at 64 seconds in DocSend's estimates, so it is the densest page here and the one to rehearse hardest. The argument in one breath: the category does monetise, just not as consumer trivia IAP. RISKS

The risks that emptied this category are the first two on this list, and they set the milestones.

  • Long-tail retention. Trivia Royale reported 45% D1 iOS — company-stated to TechCrunch, not audited — and closed eight months after launch, on 23 Feb 2021.
  • Global median D30 across 16,000+ mobile games is 0.68-0.79% (GameAnalytics est., CY2025); the North American median is 1.18%.
  • Opponent integrity. Skillz won a $42.9M jury verdict against AviaGames for willful patent infringement (9 Feb 2024); trial evidence showed bots run against paying humans.
  • Benchmark transfer. Every retention, session and per-install monetisation benchmark in our model is measured on app-store installs, not web. Our own instrument is now the route to a web-first number (§5.14, §7.3).
  • Measurement selection. The cohort rate comes only from devices that opt in to the anonymous daily ping, so its denominator is consenting devices, not all of them. We will publish it as exactly that.
  • Content depth. 54 sample questions is a demo bank. The ask funds four engineers and no separate content line; the target and production rate are still to be set (§4.4).
  • Model honesty. Our base case (P) implicitly out-monetises a 9.03%-conversion subscription at Sporcle's $43.99/yr by 3.88x. We flag it, not hide it.
Speaker notes
Each risk has a named response rather than a reassurance. Long-tail retention: the structural fact is that at a median D30 of 0.68-0.79% a game's MAU is almost entirely the current month's new installs — in our own base case the retained resident base is 5,900 of 105,900 MAU (P), 5.6% — so retention is not a metric in this category, it is the business, and that is why the entire raise is pointed at it. Note that the 0.68-0.79% band is GameAnalytics' global all-genre median, not a trivia-specific figure, and that the model's base case uses the North American 1.18%. Trivia Royale's 45% D1 is a company statement to TechCrunch, not audited; its closure was announced on 23 Feb 2021 (numbers.md §2.3). Opponent integrity: the $42.9M award was a jury verdict for willful infringement of a Skillz platform patent, not damages awarded for running bots — the bots named 'Cucumbers' and 'Guides' were trial evidence — and it is a verdict rather than a collected settlement. Our own answer to it is milestone five in numbers.md §4.4: an opponent-integrity answer ships before monetisation. Do not describe bot internals that an investor cannot verify in the repository. Benchmark transfer is the largest methodological risk in the whole model and is stated rather than buried: we have no verified benchmark for web-first game retention, web-first acquisition cost or web-first payer conversion, numbers.md §7.3 confirms §5.14 stands unchanged, and the cohort the round buys is what replaces the assumption with data — our own instrument is now the route to a web-first number rather than a request for one. Measurement selection is a risk we are raising against ourselves rather than waiting to be asked about: the cohort report is built on an opt-in anonymous daily ping (numbers.md §7.1), so its denominator is devices that consented, an opt-in sample is not a whole-population one, and the honest presentation of the first cohort number names the consent rate next to the retention rate. Do not offer a correction factor: we have no evidence base for one. The countervailing design choices are worth saying in the same breath — aggregate-only reporting, no per-device row, and suppression of any bucket describing fewer than five devices — because they are the reason the ping can be opt-in and anonymous at all, and appendix G documents them. Note the scope — it is every benchmark in numbers.md §3; Duolingo's 9.03% MAU-to-paid and Sporcle's $43.99/yr web price are not store-measured. Content depth: there is no separate content budget in §4.5, which funds four engineers and nothing else, and §4.4 milestone 4 still requires us to set a target and a production rate. If an investor adds a risk we have not listed, that is a good outcome — write it down and answer it in the follow-up, not on the spot.

APPENDIX — THE MEASUREMENT LAYER

Appendix G — how retention is measured, what is deliberately not measured, and why the rate still needs players.

  • Per device: install day, active days, session count, engaged time, and four counters — duel rounds, matches, expedition cards, quests (lib/analytics.mjs, 24 tests).
  • Per device: D1 / D7 / D30 as true, false, or null while the window is still open. A test asserts that an unfinished window can never read as a miss.
  • One device is a sample of one. No export is shaped like a rate, and the screen says so in its own copy — a rate needs a cohort.
  • Privacy: no third-party SDK, nothing personal, and the analytics module makes no network call. The player sees the whole record at /analytics and can export it as JSON or CSV, or erase it.
  • The cohort rate comes from an opt-in anonymous daily ping (cohort-ping / cohort-report, table cohort_days, 20 tests). The report is aggregate-only: no per-device row, and buckets under five devices are suppressed.
  • What none of this gives us today: a retention rate. That needs players. The instrument is what shipped; the cohort is what the round buys.
Speaker notes
This page exists so that the claim on slide 9 can be interrogated rather than believed. It is numbers.md §7.1 in slide form and it should be read as a design, not as a result. The device-local half is a pure module with no storage, no network call and no third-party SDK, which is why the privacy properties are checkable by reading one file rather than by trusting a vendor: it records install day, the days the device was active, session count under a stated inactivity threshold that the app names on screen, engaged time counted only while the app is on screen, and four in-app counters, then answers whether this device came back on day 1, 7 or 30. Both of those conventions are chosen rather than standard and the app names them on screen; say so before someone asks how a session is defined. The null return is the part worth dwelling on with a numerate investor — a window that has not finished reads as null rather than as a miss, which is the single easiest way to flatter or wreck an early retention number, and a test asserts it. The cohort half is the only part that can ever produce a rate, it is opt-in, the identifier is anonymous, the report can return no per-device row by construction, and any bucket describing fewer than five devices is suppressed rather than rounded — which is also a real limit on how early the first number can be published, and it is deliberate. The corresponding weakness is on slide 12 as a named risk: an opt-in denominator is consenting devices, not all devices, and the first published cohort figure has to carry its consent rate next to it. Twenty-four tests cover the device module and twenty the cohort endpoints; quote those two counts only, because they are the two that numbers.md §7.1 records. Finally, the honest close: none of this is traction and none of it is a number. §7.3 is explicit that there are still no users, no revenue, no cohort, no CAC, no LTV and no ARPDAU, and that every figure in §4 remains a plan.
