# FACT//DUEL Product Market Decisions

## Positioning and launch decision

Build a focused knowledge game around **“Pick your obsession. Challenge someone. Leave knowing more.”** The first product should serve people who enjoy proving specific knowledge to friends, with a satisfying explanation after the result. Sports history supplies identity and rivalry; science supplies curiosity and discovery. This is a product hypothesis, not evidence that the combined audience already wants the game.

The executive decision is to retain one recognizable duel interaction and make topic selection, readable answers, trustworthy results and rematches excellent. Launch research in three wedges: India cricket enthusiasts, European football supporters, and US-led space/science enthusiasts. Keep US basketball and American football available as expansion decks, but do not spread the initial acquisition experiment across every sport. The niche advantage must come from editorial depth and a coherent experience, not the number of menus.

Evidence was checked on **12 September 2026**. Competitor descriptions below come from current developer listings and official help pages; no private retention data or proprietary code was inspected. Market arithmetic is explicitly hypothetical. The current private prototype, its sample bank and bot sessions do not constitute demonstrated market demand.

The combined opportunity is **specialist rivalry with an explanation worth keeping**. Each component has precedents. Whether the combination earns preference requires observed behavior against the alternatives. Do not claim novelty, market leadership or “scientifically addictive” design.

The home screen should offer a primary practice action, an equally clear friend-room action, and three editorial collections. Show the selected topic, mode and opponent type before starting. After a round, prioritize the outcome, correct answer and short explanation; place one rematch action nearby. Coins remain clearly fictional and secondary to knowledge. Avoid interrupting this loop with multiple reward dialogs. This is an executive design choice to validate, not a proven universal rule.

## TAM, SAM and obtainable cohort

There is **no measured FACT//DUEL TAM or SAM yet**. The following user scenarios preserve the prior model for continuity and expose the assumptions that research must replace. They are not forecasts, confidence intervals, registrations or revenue estimates.

Define future global user TAM as online adults who would choose a free sports/science trivia product with sufficient languages and content. The formula is `6,000 million × 75% assumed adult share × joint product-interest rate`. Joint interest includes both subject interest and willingness to play this format; it avoids adding sports fans to science enthusiasts. Interest rates of 5%, 10% and 20% imply **225m, 450m and 900m** respectively. The generous language/content premise is a future possibility, not today's capability.

Define planning SAM as US, India, EU27 and UK residents who are online adults, comfortable reading the actual English cards, and interested in the product: `Σ population × online fraction × adult fraction among online users × English readiness × joint interest`. “Europe” means EU27 plus UK here; other European countries are excluded. No sport-fandom percentage is multiplied into the model.

| Regional input | US | India | EU27 | UK |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Population, millions; 2025 | 341.785 | 1,460 rounded | 451.127 | 69.487 |
| Internet fraction | 95%, 2024 carry-forward | 70%, 2025 rounded | **92% assumed** | 95%, 2024 carry-forward |
| Adult share among online users, assumed | 80% | 75% | 82% | 82% |
| English readiness, lower/base/upper assumed | 85/90/95% | 10/18/30% | 30/45/60% | 90/95/98% |
| Joint product interest, lower/base/upper assumed | 5/10/20% | 5/10/20% | 5/10/20% | 5/10/20% |

Population and available internet inputs were rechecked on World Bank country pages: [US](https://data.worldbank.org/country/united-states), [India](https://data.worldbank.org/country/india), [EU](https://data.worldbank.org/country/european-union), [UK](https://data.worldbank.org/country/united-kingdom). The EU page displayed no aggregate internet value. All adult shares, English readiness and product-interest rates are assumptions; conversational English surveys cannot establish rapid specialist reading ability.

| Potential users, millions | Lower scenario | Base scenario | Upper scenario |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Global future user TAM | 225.0 | 450.0 | 900.0 |
| US planning SAM | 11.0 | 23.4 | 49.4 |
| India planning SAM | 3.8 | 13.8 | 46.0 |
| EU27 planning SAM | 5.1 | 15.3 | 40.8 |
| UK planning SAM | 2.4 | 5.1 | 10.6 |
| **Combined planning SAM** | **22.4** | **57.6** | **146.8** |

Arithmetic precision is not measurement confidence. Halving base joint interest halves SAM to approximately **28.8m**. At base interest, reducing India English readiness from 18% to 10% lowers total SAM by approximately **6.1m**. A different reading-speed requirement could therefore change the reachable audience more than a visual theme. Appropriate device access, editorial coverage and actual product availability still limit serviceability. The private site's accessible cohort is much smaller than these future scenarios.

SOM should start from distribution actually secured. For an illustrative six-week recruitment effort, define retained pilot users as distinct eligible prospects reached through permitted communities × first-match completion × return in days 22–28. Deduplicate people belonging to several communities. Every value below is an assumed planning input; no communities or users have been secured by this research.

| Scenario | Communities | Distinct prospects each | First-match completion | Week-four return among activated | Retained users implied |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Small | 5 | 40 | 35% | 20% | 14 |
| Base | 15 | 80 | 45% | 25% | 135 |
| Wider | 30 | 150 | 55% | 35% | About 866 |

The product currently has no paid offering, so current transaction revenue is zero. A hypothetical dollar TAM would add unsupported payer and pricing assumptions. Consider ad-free presentation, cosmetic themes or creator hosting tools only after repeat play is demonstrated. Do not finance the experience through paid answer advantages or inflated fictional balances.

## Three acquisition wedges

| Wedge | First editorial collection | Recruitment and product hypothesis | Expansion decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **India cricket friends** | India ODI/T20 history, completed World Cups, IPL/WPL moments, players and venues; separate formats and eras | Test whether a friend-room invitation settles familiar debates and prompts voluntary rematches. Recruit through explicitly permitted adult fan and alumni groups. | Add Hindi or another requested language only after observing English reading friction and recruiting language reviewers. |
| **European football supporters** | World Cups, Euros, Champions League and selected club histories, including women's competitions | Test whether a recognizable club/era choice creates stronger preference than random broad trivia. Recruit permitted supporter groups; avoid implying club endorsement. | Expand club filters only when each advertised deck has enough reviewed facts to prevent immediate repetition. |
| **US-led space and science curiosity** | Solar system, missions, light, motion and surprising life-science basics; optional deeper branches | Test whether a satisfying explanation creates repeat visits even after losing. Recruit consenting astronomy, maker and science-education communities. | Add chemistry, computing, awards and advanced physics according to observed choice and source coverage; test US basketball/NFL collections separately. |

Use the same visual system across wedges, with editorial imagery and accent changes that preserve predictable answer colors and controls. Test one meaningful proposition per recruitment page. A combined sports-science crossover collection can explore ball flight, friction, reaction time and materials, but should earn its place through play data.

Practice bots solve the empty-room experience, not multiplayer liquidity. Label random guessing and randomized response timing honestly; do not present a random bot as a calibrated expert. Track bot play separately from human invitations, accepted challenges and completed human matches. Keep topic choice broad enough for discovery while avoiding dozens of empty public queues.

## Decision gates and progress tracking

The thresholds below are **management hypotheses**, not industry benchmarks. Start with moderated sessions, then recruit at least 100 activated participants per wedge across multiple communities before deciding where to invest. Report uncertainty and recruitment bias; small differences are not proof of a winning segment.

| Gate and owner | Evidence to collect | Proposed go condition | Stop or revise condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-use clarity — UX lead | Observe first practice match; ask player to explain timer, wrong answers and bot identity | At least 8/10 moderated participants complete without assistance and explain the basic rule | Repeated confusion: simplify setup before adding modes |
| Activation — product analyst | Completed first match / eligible unique starters | At least 70% in each pilot wedge | Below 50% after one repair cycle: investigate loading, reading and setup friction |
| Repeat value — product lead | Return in days 7–13; bot and human cohorts separate | At least 25% of activated players return, with qualitative reasons consistent with the proposition | Below 15% after two content/flow iterations: pause expansion |
| Social value — growth lead | Voluntary friend invitations and accepted-room completion | At least 15% invite; at least 50% of accepted invitations become completed matches | High sharing but failed matches: repair access/room flow before acquisition |
| Trust — editorial and engineering leads | Verified answer errors; disputes per 100 completed rounds; explained timing outcomes | No known critical factual errors remain; at least 90% in a comprehension sample can interpret results | Unexplained outcomes, misleading bot identity or unresolved critical errors block cohort growth |
| Editorial sustainability — bank lead | Review throughput, correction age, unseen-question coverage | Each promoted narrow deck has at least 40 reviewed fact families and an assigned maintainer | Repetition or unreviewed automated material: reduce advertised scope |

Instrument consented events for setup start, topic choice, match start/completion, opponent type, invitation acceptance, rematch, explanation opening and question dispute. Count people, matches and rounds separately. Do not manufacture online-player counts, global rankings, measured mastery or cohort retention from local storage, bot sessions or internal tests.

For progress tracking, each roadmap issue needs one accountable owner, hypothesis, dependency, acceptance evidence, status and decision date. The next investment decision should follow real completion, trust and return behavior. TAM remains context; the experiment is whether a small group voluntarily chooses a second and third session.
