# Jaanta Hai Kya — Club Pavilion

A mobile-first cricket knowledge rivalry game with a personal stadium, collectible identity and sourced outside-the-pitch stories. The first useful task is playing, in one tap. No basic cricket-law recall in the served bank. Spicy is a public-record category, not a gossip generator.

Primary flows: one-tap Mini / Full cricket practice, one-tap Spicy, human/friend duels, clubs of up to 22, playing sides of five or eleven, asynchronous club wars and four-team cups, a personal local club, Net practice, and sourced answers. Play / Teams / Explore / My club / Answers form the five mobile destinations. Day and night are visual worlds of the same app. “Gyaan hai? Game dikha.” is the short Hinglish challenge on the home page; mode names, bot disclosure and reward conditions remain literal. Verified club membership adds its public tag before a player name and can apply the club’s teal, saffron, berry or blue accent throughout the app. Use club colours is an explicit setting; day/night and motion preferences remain independent.

Team play is a separate server record using the existing email-free human profile. Each club has up to 22 members and a fixed playing format of five or eleven. Selected players contribute; reserves remain outside that match's score. A Captain controls the club; a Vice Captain can arrange fixtures and a Coach can select lineups. Fun tags require recipient opt-in and grant no permissions.

Accepted new fixtures open 23 hours of preparation and 24 hours of battle. Preparation permits substitutions; battle freezes player identities and forbids replacements. Both Captain/VC sides can confirm readiness for an early start; a lineup edit clears readiness. Each selected player contributes a timed Mini or Full over. A four-team cup has two semi-finals and one final, with preparation before each new stage. Legacy fixtures retain their existing clocks and frozen lineups. Weekly recurrence creates an inert rematch draft: the home side must send a fresh sheet and the away side accept a fresh sheet. Either club can stop the series. No background scheduler, unattended acceptance or missed-cycle backlog is promised.

Public club tags identify teams; private invite codes grant membership. Team points unlock shared levels, original crests and a Legacy frame; they do not change quiz rules or personal balances. Recruiting cups can be left/cancelled and expire after 48 hours. Cricket outcome moments use original bat/ball/bails art and synthetic cheers after settlement only, are skippable, and respect sound, volume and motion preferences.

GitHub Pages serves frontend and offline-capable practice; an isolated Supabase authority owns human matches, coins and rankings. Club points and badges are explicitly local, free-earned cosmetics. Coins and Club points cannot be bought, cashed out or combined. Preset opponent stickers are delivered by the match authority with a cooldown, clear mute control and no overlay on answers.

Acceptance: one click to questions; all views reachable; real/stale online population stated truthfully; no duplicate answer, reward or purchase; source-backed dramatic facts; actual day/night art; stable timed layout and reduced-motion equivalent; default-off ads; tested production build and deployed backend compatibility. Automated emulation is not a claim of measured user retention or physical-device testing.

Obsidian polish adds subtle static reflections and finite navigation/interaction glints on dark chrome, with opaque fallbacks and motion guards. Answer cards and timed clocks remain stable. Club display data is a public whitelist (tag/name/palette/playing size), never an invite code or permission assertion; stale identity responses cannot keep another club’s colours after a profile change. Design/research and stress evidence are in docs/obsidian-polish/.
